I live near Margaret River, on a property that has been a group settlement block, a dairy, an organic winery and now a shared family farm. I run an aquaponics greenhouse there. I also take photographs, write software, build electronics, and occasionally construct rooms that project images onto four walls at once.

Photography is not a hobby parked beside the rest. It is one of the ways I look at a place or a problem — underwater and coastal work, documentary pictures, historical projects — and sometimes the pictures become the project. The Georgette wreck is the clearest case: years of photographs, then research, then an exhibition, then the software the exhibition required.

That list looks scattered until you watch what actually happens. A farm needs water-quality data, so sensors appear. A veterinary workflow needs structured reports, so an application appears. A shipwreck on a local beach turns into pictures, then a book, then a room. The medium keeps changing. The habit doesn’t.

I have a long history of solving practical problems, including years of veterinary work. That background is useful. It teaches you to look carefully, not to invent a more complicated story than the evidence supports, and to build tools around the way people already work rather than the way a product manager might prefer them to work.

> The common thread isn’t the medium. It’s curiosity and solving the problem.

I am a practical and creative generalist. Arduino and Raspberry Pi boards, wireless sensor networks, AI-assisted development, photogrammetry, Unreal Engine, MetaHumans, drones, underwater cameras — I pick these up because a particular problem has made them necessary, then keep going when they turn out to be interesting in their own right.

I am not trying to be a specialist in one narrow profession. Breadth is the point, as long as it stays attached to a real constraint: a greenhouse that has to stay alive, a report that a colleague has to finish, a visitor standing in a dark room who should understand something they didn’t understand when they walked in.

If you want the work rather than the biography, start with [the photographs](/photography) or [the projects](/projects). One current example is [building the Georgette 150th exhibition](/projects/ss-georgette-150th); the public exhibition is at [exhibition.margies.app](https://exhibition.margies.app/). This site — margies.app — is the root for everything else.
