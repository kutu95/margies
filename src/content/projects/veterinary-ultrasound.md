Ultrasound reporting in veterinary work is a specialised workflow. The examination has a shape. Measurements have to be captured in the right place. Images have to stay attached to the right study. The report that leaves the room has to be readable by another clinician, not just by the person who wrote it.

Generic medical software is rarely designed for that shape. It is designed for a hospital IT list, or for a human clinic, or for a vendor’s idea of a “case”. I built a custom application around the examination as it is actually performed.

## What the system is

This is a web application for recording a veterinary ultrasound study and producing the report. It supports structured examination entry, measurements, image handling and the generation of a finished document. The aim was to reduce the gap between what happened in the scan and what ended up on paper — and to stop people reconstructing a study from memory at the end of a long day.

It is internal software. It is not a public product, and this page is not an advertisement for veterinary diagnosis or treatment. I have a veterinary background; that is why I understood the workflow. The portfolio claim is about the software.

## Why custom

Off-the-shelf systems tend to be either too broad or too rigid. Too broad, and every field is optional, so nothing is consistent. Too rigid, and the software argues with the way a particular practice actually works. A small, specific application can insist on the structure that matters and stay out of the way for the rest.

Report generation is the other half. A report that is only a dump of fields is not useful. A report that is only a prose essay is hard to compare with the last one. The system had to produce something a colleague could read quickly and trust.

## What it demonstrates

Web application development attached to a real professional workflow. Structured data. Images that must not get lost. An interface that can be used under time pressure. Those constraints are more instructive than a demo app with tidy sample data.
